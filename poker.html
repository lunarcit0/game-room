BEFORE YOU DRAW

What are the odds?

Try a question about your opening hand. Practice is optional.

Only your opening five cards are known. The opponent’s cards stay unknown, so the sample space has 47 unseen cards. Discards are not returned to the deck.

How to play

Each hand uses a freshly shuffled 52-card deck with no jokers. You and the opponent receive five cards each. Click up to three of yours, then draw replacements—or keep all five. The opponent makes one exchange too. The stronger final hand wins.

This table uses a maximum of three replacements. There is no betting or money. Each hand is independent, and session counts reset when you leave.

The opponent keeps made straights and flushes, keeps matching ranks, tries four-card flushes or straights, and otherwise keeps its two highest cards. It only considers its own cards.

Probability questions refer to your draw before the opponent exchanges. Selecting no replacements makes the question a hypothetical one-card draw.

Hand rankings & ties
  1. Straight flush (royal flush is the highest)
  2. Four of a kind
  3. Full house
  4. Flush
  5. Straight
  6. Three of a kind
  7. Two pair
  8. One pair
  9. High card

Higher ranks and then kickers break ties. Suits have equal value. An ace is high except in A–2–3–4–5, where the straight is five-high.
